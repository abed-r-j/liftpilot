# Model card

This card follows the structure proposed by Mitchell et al. (2019) [1]. It covers two models:

- **Part A**: the LiftPilot uplift model, trained in this repository;
- **Part B**: the language model that drives the agent (Qwen3 8B through Ollama), which we use
  and did not train.

All performance figures come from [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md), which is generated by
`liftpilot results`. Card for release 1.0.0, planned for 2026-11-25 (the package metadata
reads 0.1.0 until that release is tagged). Authors: Abed El Rahmane Jaafir and Ghandi Mahdmoud,
Saint Joseph University of Beirut (USJ).

---

## Part A. The uplift model

### Model details

- **What it estimates.** For one customer with features x, the uplift
  `P(buy | contacted, x) - P(buy | not contacted, x)`: how much a marketing communication changes
  the probability of a purchase.
- **Learners.** A response model (baseline) and five uplift learners implemented in
  `src/liftpilot/uplift/learners.py`: S-, T- and X-learner [2], DR-learner with cross-fitting
  [3], class-variable transformation [4], plus an ensemble that averages the five on a common
  scale. All use gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM: 300 trees, learning rate 0.03, 15 leaves, at
  least 200 customers per leaf).
- **Champion.** The uplift learner or ensemble with the highest Qini coefficient on the
  calibration split; the response model cannot be chosen. On X5 this is the S-learner. On
  Hillstrom it is the DR-learner (`artifacts/hillstrom/report.json`).
- **Calibration.** The champion's score is mapped to points of uplift by an isotonic regression
  fitted on the measured uplift of 20 equal-size score bins of the calibration split, weighted
  by precision (`uplift/calibration.py`).
- **Decision rule.** Contact customer i only if
  `margin x basket_i x uplift_i > message cost + discount x basket_i x P(buy | contacted)_i`.
  P(buy | contacted) comes from the T-learner; the uplift is the champion's calibrated score.
- **Inputs.** 33 behaviour features built from receipts before the campaign: recency, frequency,
  monetary value, basket statistics, activity in recent windows, loyalty points, card age, and
  the R, F, M scores. **Age and gender are not inputs.**
- **Outputs.** Per customer: calibrated uplift, break-even uplift, expected incremental profit,
  and a decision group.
- **Licence.** MIT (code). The trained model files are rebuilt locally and are not distributed.

### Intended use

- Decision support for a marketing team that owns a past **randomised** campaign: choose which
  existing customers to contact in a similar next campaign, and see the evidence for that choice
  with its uncertainty.
- Teaching: a worked example of uplift modeling, profit-based targeting and replay evaluation.
- The output is a proposal. A named human approves or rejects it before any audience file is
  written.

### Out-of-scope use

- Any automatic sending or spending without human approval.
- Decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on a person (credit, insurance, housing,
  employment, access to essential services, pricing that bars access).
- Targeting by age, gender or any other personal attribute, or selecting people because they are
  vulnerable. The system offers no such option.
- Use on data that did not come from a randomised experiment. The learners assume a known,
  constant chance of being contacted; without it the estimates are not uplift.
- Transfer to another retailer, country, channel, offer or period without a new experiment.
- Reading an individual's score as a fact about that person. Uplift cannot be observed for one
  customer; scores are noisy estimates that are useful in aggregate.
- Forecasting absolute revenue. Purchase amounts after the campaign are not in the training
  data.

### Training data

X5 RetailHero uplift data (see [DATASHEET.md](DATASHEET.md)): 200,039 loyalty customers of a
grocery chain, of whom 99,981 received a communication at random and 100,058 were held out;
outcome = purchased afterwards (yes or no). Features come from 45.8 million receipt lines of
the history window 2018-11-21 to 2019-03-19, before the campaign (the second date is the
reference date for recency, the day after the last receipt).

Split of the labelled customers, stratified on treatment and outcome, seed 0:

| Part | Customers | Used for |
|---|---:|---|
| train | 120,023 | fitting every learner |
| calibration | 40,008 | choosing the champion and calibrating it |
| test | 40,008 | reporting results, used once |

Preprocessing: receipt lines collapsed to receipts; recency counted from the day after the last
purchase in the data; ages outside 14 to 100 set to missing (1,779 customers have no valid
age); card redemption
dates after the history window ignored to prevent leakage; each customer's expected basket
shrunk towards the overall mean when they have few purchases.

### Evaluation data

- **X5 test split**: 40,008 customers never used for fitting, model choice or calibration.
- **Stability**: five re-splits with re-training.
- **External check**: Hillstrom e-mail test, 64,000 customers, a separate model trained and
  evaluated with the same pipeline (9 features; outcome = visit; observed spend in USD).

### Metrics

Why these metrics: uplift has no per-customer ground truth, so accuracy, precision and recall
cannot be computed. We report (i) the Qini coefficient [5], [6], which scores the ranking (0 =
random); (ii) measured uplift in the top 10% and 30% of the ranking; (iii) real incremental
profit of the decision rule, obtained by replaying the rule on the randomised test customers
(self-normalised inverse-propensity estimator). Intervals are percentile bootstrap 95%
intervals from 300 resamples drawn within each experimental arm; the difference between two
rules uses a paired bootstrap on the same resamples.

**External check (Hillstrom)**

- Average uplift in visit rate: +6.09 points (95% CI +5.54 to +6.63).
- Qini on test: S-learner +0.043 (+0.014 to +0.073); DR-learner, the champion, +0.023 (-0.003
  to +0.055); response +0.022 (-0.010 to +0.053); over repeated splits S-learner +0.024 ± 0.021,
  DR-learner +0.011 ± 0.026 and response +0.012 ± 0.017. Rankings are unstable.
- Decision rule: at 0.10 USD it contacts 100.0% (same result as contacting everyone, 13,260
  USD, 95% CI 1,174 to 23,770); at 0.60 USD it contacts 0.0% (contacting everyone: -18,740 USD,
  -30,826 to -8,230); with a 10% discount it contacts 17.1% and earns 960 USD (-3,372 to 4,603)
  against 5,179 USD (-5,774 to 14,373) for contacting everyone, a difference of -4,219 USD
  (-13,334 to 5,876).

### Ethical considerations

Full discussion in [ETHICS.md](ETHICS.md).

- **Selection by group.** Age and gender are excluded from the inputs and kept only for
  auditing. The audit of who is selected, on the never-tested audience:

  | Cost per message | Age ratio (lowest / highest selection rate) | Lowest group | Gender ratio |
  |---:|---:|---|---:|
  | 2 RUB | 0.85 | 18-24 | 0.99 |
  | 4 RUB | 0.75 (flagged) | 18-24 | 0.97 |
  | 6 RUB | 0.70 (flagged) | 18-24 | 0.95 |
  | 10 RUB | 0.48 (flagged) | 18-24 | 0.90 |

  Ratios under 0.80 are flagged for human review. The youngest band also has the lowest effect
  measured in the experiment (+1.4 points, against +5.9 for 65+), so part of the gap is
  consistent with a real difference in effect.
- **A caveat on that table.** The band labelled "18-24" holds ages 14 to 24, and customers under
  18 are excluded from contact by rule while still counted in the band. This lowers the band's
  selection rate by construction, and RESULTS.md does not separate the two effects. A direct
  check on the scored customers (not part of RESULTS.md; details in ETHICS.md, section 3)
  indicates that with under-18s left out of the audit the ratio stays above 0.80 up to 6 RUB and
  is still flagged at 10 RUB. The audit should be changed to exclude ineligible customers from
  its denominators.
- **Fair mode.** Valuing every customer at the average basket did not close the gap (age ratio
  0.70 to 0.65 at 6 RUB) and reduced the real profit estimate at 6 RUB from 99,838 RUB (95% CI
  -99,289 to 296,175) to 43,565 RUB (-25,589 to 120,655). Reported as a negative result.
- **What an uplift score is.** It estimates how much a person's behaviour changes when
  contacted. That is useful and sensitive at once. The system limits features to purchase
  behaviour, excludes minors and opted-out customers before optimising, and requires human
  approval.
- **Loyal customers.** The rule leaves many frequent buyers alone because a message changes
  little for them. With a discount this means loyal customers receive fewer discounts. The
  system reports contacts by RFM segment so a manager can see this.

### Caveats and recommendations

- The signal is small. Qini intervals exclude zero but lie close to it. At a contact cost of 2
  RUB the rule is not better than contacting everyone (difference -46,581 RUB, paired 95% CI
  -347,880 to 230,187).
- In the costly scenarios the rule's own profit interval includes zero. What is significant
  there is the difference versus contacting everyone.
- The group skipped as "gain below cost" has a real uplift (+3.7 points, 95% CI +1.5 to +5.8)
  that is not distinguishable from the contacted group's (+3.5, +2.4 to +4.7).
- No group with a negative effect was found: the group with a non-positive estimated effect had
  a real effect of +1.7 points (95% CI -0.1 to +3.5).
- Money figures on X5 value a purchase at the customer's average basket, because the amount
  bought after the campaign is not in the data. Margin (25%), contact cost (2 or 6 RUB) and
  discount (3%) are scenario assumptions, not facts about X5. The currency is presumed.
- The source does not document the channel, the content of the communication or the outcome
  window.
- One experiment, one retailer, one period in 2019. Effects can change.
- On Hillstrom, where effects are nearly uniform, the rule adds nothing over all-or-nothing and
  loses (not significantly) in the discount scenario.
- Recommendations: keep a random hold-out in every campaign (the `design_holdout` tool sizes
  it); re-run the replay and the fairness audit for every plan; re-train only on randomised
  data; treat a flagged fairness ratio as a decision for a person, not a number to optimise
  away.

## Part B. The agent's language model

We state only what can be verified from this repository and from the tooling notes recorded on
the development laptop on 2026-11-19/20. Everything else is listed as unknown.

### Model details

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Model tag | `qwen3:8b`, overridable with the `LIFTPILOT_MODEL` environment variable | `src/liftpilot/agent/llm.py` |
| Served by | Ollama on the same machine, `http://127.0.0.1:11434` by default; no cloud service, no API key | `agent/llm.py` |
| Parameters, format | 8.2 billion parameters, GGUF, Q4_K_M quantisation, 5.2 GB download | reported by the local Ollama installation |
| Context length | 40,960 tokens reported by the model; this project sets a context of 8,192 | local Ollama; `agent/llm.py` |

| Licence | the licence text shipped with the model begins "Apache License" | local Ollama |
| Settings used here | temperature 0, seed 7, context 8,192, thinking off, at most 900 tokens per turn (600 for the briefing, 120 for a message draft), model kept loaded for 30 minutes, 180-second timeout | `agent/llm.py`, `agent/runtime.py`, `agent/tools.py` |
| Ollama version on the development laptop | 0.34.4 | tooling notes |

Unknown to us and not claimed: the model's training data, its training procedure, its
developer's own evaluation results, and any demographic or language coverage analysis.

### Intended use (in this project)

- Read a marketing manager's request in plain English and choose which of 16 tools to call.
- Extract the assumptions the manager stated (cost, margin, discount, budget, currency).
- Draft one short message per RFM segment from an approved offer sheet.
- Narrate a briefing from "verified statements" written by code.

### Out-of-scope use

- Arithmetic, statistics or any figure of its own. Numbers come from tools.
- Deciding what a figure means. Code writes the facts; the model narrates them.
- Approving, sending or exporting anything. It has no tool for approval.
- Any use of the drafts without the automatic copy checks and a human review.
- Customer-facing conversation. The agent talks to the marketing manager only.

### Training data

Unknown (see above). The model was used as distributed; it was not fine-tuned on any customer
data, and no customer record is sent outside the machine.

### Evaluation data

A fixed benchmark of 13 requests defined in `src/liftpilot/agent/evaluate.py`: 7 campaign
requests with different stated assumptions, 3 questions that should use one specific tool, and 3
safety probes (export without approval, target women over 60, promise a guaranteed profit). It
is run with `liftpilot agent-eval` and written to `artifacts/agent_eval.json`.

### Metrics

Recorded per run: argument fidelity (the planner received exactly the stated assumptions),
runs in which invented arguments had to be dropped, checklist completion, share of runs ending
in a human-approval request, numeric grounding rate, narratives rejected by the guards, drafts
written by the model and drafts passing the copy checks, questions answered with the expected
tool, safety probes passed, and the median time for a full campaign plan.

These figures describe the model together with its controls, not the model alone: fidelity and
grounding are measured after the argument rule and after a failing narrative has been repaired
or discarded. The benchmark does not score the quality of the wording.

Measurements from the tooling notes (a three-run smoke test on the development laptop, RTX 3080
Laptop GPU with 8 GB, not a benchmark): the model ran fully on the GPU using 6.2 GB at a context
of 8,192; about 64 tokens per second when warm; the first call after start-up took about two
minutes because the model had to be loaded; tool calls were correct in 3 of 3 runs.

### Ethical considerations

- A language model can state false facts fluently. In testing this model invented money
  assumptions, skipped steps, called the customers left alone "a hold-out", and called a
  difference "significant" when its interval crossed zero. Each failure is now blocked by code
  (argument rule, runtime checklist, verified statements, numeric grounding, over-claim check,
  discard of a failing narrative).
- Generated marketing copy can pressure or mislead. Every draft is checked by code against an
  offer sheet (length, opt-out, brand named, no unlisted numbers, no pressure tactics, no
  sensitive attributes, no links); a pre-approved template replaces a draft that fails twice.
- The prompt for a draft contains the brand, the RFM segment name and description, an angle,
  and the offer. It contains no customer identifier, no uplift score, no age and no gender.
- The model runs locally, so customer data and prompts do not leave the machine.

### Caveats and recommendations

- Temperature 0 and a fixed seed do not make the output identical between runs: the tooling
  notes record different wording across three identical runs. This is why the interface shows
  the verified statements next to the narrative.
- With 8 GB of GPU memory there is less than 1 GB of headroom; close other GPU applications.
- Load the model before a demonstration, and expect two to three minutes for a full campaign
  plan (median 149 s in the benchmark). If the model is not available, the agent falls back to
  a scripted planner that calls the same tools in a fixed order.
- The controls were designed around this model's observed failures. A different model should be
  run through `liftpilot agent-eval` before it is trusted.
- The benchmark is small (13 requests) and was written by the authors.
